## PPBE

Timing: post. Coverage: all participants.

User-supplied literature-grounded adaptation: Hamm et al. (2023); Wang & Benbasat (2016); traceability/inspectability wording grounded in Nauta et al. (2023) and Kulesza et al. (2015).

- PPBE_1: The assistant made clear which evidence its recommendation was based on.
- PPBE_2: I could tell which parts of the source documents the recommendation came from.
- PPBE_3: The evidence behind the recommendation was available for me to examine.
- PPBE_4: The assistant showed enough of its supporting evidence for the recommendation to be checked.
- PPBE_5: It was unclear what evidence the recommendation actually relied on. **Reverse-scored.**

Response range: 1–7; Strongly disagree to Strongly agree.

## ANT

Timing: post. Coverage: all participants.

User-supplied adaptation: Klein & Martinez (2023), Jensen et al. (2020), Bartneck et al. (2009). Five human-likeness items; no Gefen & Straub social-presence items.

- ANT_1: Using this assistant gives me the feeling of personal communication.
- ANT_2: This assistant behaves human-like.
- ANT_3: This assistant shows human characteristics.
- ANT_4: The assistant communicated with me like a human would.
- ANT_5: The assistant's communication seemed human-like rather than machine-like.

Response range: 1–7; Strongly disagree to Strongly agree.

## TRUST

Timing: post. Coverage: all participants.

User-supplied task adaptation: Benbasat & Wang (2005), with Mayer et al. (1995) as theoretical basis. Expertise/ability and integrity-related judgements.

- TRUST_1: This assistant is like a real expert in assessing candidates.
- TRUST_2: This assistant has the expertise to assess candidates accurately.
- TRUST_3: This assistant has the ability to understand the requirements relevant to the assessment.
- TRUST_4: This assistant has good knowledge of the recruitment decision domain.
- TRUST_5: This assistant puts making an accurate assessment first.
- TRUST_6: This assistant keeps a fair assessment in mind.
- TRUST_7: This assistant provides unbiased recommendations.
- TRUST_8: This assistant is honest.
- TRUST_9: I consider this assistant to possess integrity.

Response range: 1–7; Strongly disagree to Strongly agree.

## PCE

Timing: post. Coverage: forcing_only.

Candidate contextual adaptation: Rotgans and Schmidt (2011); Drosos et al. (2025); Fischer and Schraffenberger (2025). Task-specific independent evaluation wording; not an established cognitive-forcing scale.

- PCE_1: Re-entering the role requirements before the AI assessment required me to think carefully about the mandatory requirements.
- PCE_2: Re-entering the role requirements before the AI assessment required me to work out what those requirements meant for screening a candidate.
- PCE_3: Re-entering the role requirements before the AI assessment required me to evaluate the mandatory requirements for myself.

Response range: 1–7; Strongly disagree to Strongly agree.

## Interpretation and pending implementation

PCE is a candidate adapted perception measure administered only to F1 participants after all six trials. F0 values are structurally not applicable, never zero. No between-condition PCE comparison or causal mediation is specified. Completion, attempts and timing are primary forcing implementation checks.

General generosity is not risk aversion. Risk aversion and NCS6 are not part of the active agreed battery. Pre-task constructs are descriptive; adding them as covariates requires a documented prospective analysis decision.
